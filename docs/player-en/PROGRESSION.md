# Traders, quests and gun turrets

Weapons are bought from characters in the game world. There is no longer a freely accessible weapon or training menu. Inventory, pause and settings remain available at any time; **Q** shows or hides the quest tracker. In co-op, fights keep going during a conversation. In solo, the conversation pauses the game.

## Getting started at the campfire

**Mara, the forester**, waits at the small fire pit on the western field path. **E** opens her quest menu. She sells nothing and hands out her own quest line: “What the Forest Gives Us” (5 porcini, 90 R), “Unrest on the Paths” (12 runners, 140 R) and “While the Fire Burns” (wave 6 and 80 zombies, 220 R); they require mission level 2, 5 and 8. The first quest becomes available after “By the Fire”. The mushrooms stay in your inventory; team progress counts in solo and co-op. Mara appears on the minimap once you have seen her from no more than 30 m with a clear line of sight, and she gets a golden question mark when a quest is ready to turn in.

**Vendor** stands at the campfire and sells weapons, ammo, bandages and grenades. Talk to Vendor with **E**, accept “By the Fire” under **Quests** and collect the reward. **Mechanic** stands a little further north by the guidepost. She offers defense quests, training and the management of towers that are already built. Both appear on the minimap once you have seen them from no more than 30 m with a clear line of sight.

## Holding the forest hut

The forest hut has its own health (5000, shown below the wave bar). A good share of the zombies that get inside the ring go straight for its walls, and every zombie close to a wall strikes it; titan slams hit it as well. At the first hit, the warning **“WARNING: THE FOREST HUT IS UNDER ATTACK! Defend it!”** appears and the hut pulses red on the minimap. **E** at a wall repairs 500 HP of the structure; this costs 30 R in wave 1 and 5 R more with every further wave (wave 10: 75 R), and smaller repairs cost a proportional share. If the hut drops to zero, the round is lost, even if all players are still alive.

Quests are accepted and turned in at the NPC who gives them. Completed goals pay their reward exactly once. In co-op, built defenses, kills, the delivery and titan kills count for the whole team; accepting a quest, the reward, weapons and skins belong to each individual player.

Mission level = waves survived + 1; below the listed level you cannot accept a quest. The toolbox for the delivery lies in a different, random spot every round; once you accept the quest, the spot is marked on the map. A hidden trader with a stall of his own is in the northern forest. His map marker appears once you have seen him from no more than 30 m with a clear line of sight. He sells rare weapons and special finishes.

## Operating towers

- **T:** open the build preview. Green shows a valid spot; red names the obstacle.
- **R / Mouse wheel:** rotate in 15° steps. **Shift+R** rotates back.
- **E:** build, or confirm the new orientation. **T / Escape:** cancel.
- **E at an existing tower:** climb up and operate the weapon; E again climbs down. Left click fires, hold right click to aim. **R** re-aims an unoccupied tower. In automatic mode the **160° field of fire** applies; operated manually, the tower can fire all around.
- **F at a tower:** repair for 35 R. Away from towers, F still toggles the flashlight.
- **At Mechanic → Towers:** upgrade towers or dismantle your own. For the Sentinel, the tiers cost 100 / 175 R; other types scale with their build price. Upgrades increase structure and range and repair the tower at the same time. Dismantling refunds a third of the build price.

The Sentinel costs 120 R, the Flamethrower 260 R, the Mortar 380 R, the Heavy MG 450 R and the Tesla Coil 600 R. The team can build no more than six towers. The Sentinel has ranges of 26 / 32 / 38 m and structure of 240 / 400 / 600. A clear line of fire, spread, turning time and overheating still matter. Tower kills earn the builder half the normal kill value. Living teammates also receive a support share of 25% of the kill value awarded.

A barricade is built, reinforced or, when damaged, repaired right at the line with **E**. **V** points you to defense advice at Mechanic. The existing palisade and its four approaches remain part of the defense.

## Earning weapons

The Pistol is the starting weapon. You no longer get a Shotgun automatically in wave 3. Three weapons lie in the huts: an MP5 on the upper floor of the forest hut (from wave 3) plus a Shotgun (from wave 3) and a Ranger .308 (from wave 8) in the woodshed, each behind locked doors whose keys have to be found in the forest. You can only take them once the quest, purchase permit and waves survived are met, just as for buying them; they are free then, and if you already own the weapon, you get ammo instead. Crates in the forest hut and the woodshed provide one to four magazines for the currently equipped weapon, depending on the wave: one in waves 1–4, two from wave 5, three from wave 9 and four from wave 13. Every weapon purchase comes with a loaded magazine and two spare magazines; the Forest Axe needs no ammo.

You get a purchase permit once you have turned in every quest of the quest line with the same name: Assault (“Hold the Line”, “The Long Shift”), Marksman (“A Steady Hand”, “Precision Under Pressure”, “A Discreet Job”) and Defense Engineering (“The First Sentinel”, “Crossfire”, “Better Safe Than Sorry”, “Like Clockwork”). The price, wave and quest still apply on top.

The **Mouse wheel** switches between all the weapons you bought. **1–9 and 0** are the quick bar slots: at the start they only hold the Pistol (1) and the Field Knife (0); you put bought weapons on a slot in the inventory with a right click. The Ranger .308 is a slow precision rifle; the MG-60 has a large magazine but a long reload. The Nightbreaker 12 is a semi-automatic close-range weapon. The Titanbreaker .50 deals 75% bonus damage against titans, but holds only four rounds and uses expensive ammo. Both precision rifles have a stronger aiming zoom.

Ammo costs 12 / 24 / 30 / 44 / 28 / 40 / 90 / 70 / 110 R per two magazines, in the order Pistol, Revolver, MP5, AK-47, Shotgun, Ranger .308, MG-60, Nightbreaker 12, Titanbreaker .50. Reserves are limited to four magazines; the exceptions are the Pistol (eight), the Desert Eagle .50 (six), the Plasma Rifle and Graviton Cannon (five each), the Flare Pistol (14) and the Minigun M134 (two). After a wave, only the Pistol reserve is raised to at least 36 rounds. The break between waves lasts 180 seconds; Enter starts the next wave sooner. Grenades cost 45 R, a bandage for up to 60 health costs 35 R.

Skins are bought for the equipped weapon: **Forest Camo** (160 R), **Soot Bronze** (300 R) and **Titan Bone** (480 R). They change materials, not combat stats. Skins you already bought and the original finish can be applied again for free at the matching trader. Skins are visible to other co-op players too.

Weapons, quests and purchased upgrades apply to the current run. A joint restart begins with the Pistol again; the existing permanent achievements and high scores are kept.

## Balance and testing

Achievement rewards were reduced; weapon purchases no longer pay you back through achievements. Rem Dollars have to be split between weapons, ammo, defense and training. On Normal, without headshot and streak bonuses, achievements and quests, a sample of 100 wave plans per wave yields about 126 Rem Dollars after wave 1, 302 after wave 2 and 911 after wave 4, each before spending. This is a verifiable baseline balance, not a guarantee of perfect balance for every play style or difficulty.

`godot/tests/progression.gd` checks real trader distance, purchases, duplicate rewards, quest and wave locks, reserve limits, skins, tower rotation, the field of fire and a real hit with the Titanbreaker .50. `multiplayer.gd` checks four independent game processes; `packed_coop.gd` additionally checks the Windows build. `balance_report.gd` writes the sample and the weapon data to `artifacts/progression/balance.json`. Rendered test screenshots are also in `artifacts/progression/`.

All players need the same current build. The host re-checks trader actions for distance, line of sight, health, price, ownership and requirements. The version check also covers weapon and trader data and whether their models are available.

## Models

Three new NPCs and four new weapons were generated with Meshy 6. The characters have a skeleton and an idle animation. Their original normal and metallic/roughness maps were carried over into the rigged files after checking that the UV coordinates are identical. The prompts and the resumable production pipeline are in `tools/progression_assets.json` and `tools/progression_assets.py`; originals and task records stay in `meshy_output`. The game uses the compressed GLBs in `godot/assets/models`. The three weapon finishes are generated with a dedicated material shader.

### Hunting and venison

- Wild animals can be hunted; first kill: achievement **Hunter** (+25 Rem Dollars as a team achievement).
- **E** at the kill: pick up the venison (stag 4, roe deer 3, bird 1).
- **E** at the camp grill by the forest hut: grill one portion for 6 seconds. The finished meat goes into your inventory automatically.
- Click Grilled Venison in the inventory or put it on the quick bar: +35 health. At full health, no meat is used up.
- Vendor and Secret Vendor buy Raw Venison for 12 R and Grilled Venison for 20 R per portion.
- In co-op, each kill can only be looted once; meat supplies and grilling belong to each individual player.

### Golden Bolete

An extremely rare forest find: 5% chance per round, at most one at a random free spot. Collect it with **E** and sell it to **Vendor** or **Secret Vendor** under **Sell** for **1000 Rem Dollars**. A pure sale item, not edible. In co-op, all players share the same find, which can be collected only once.
